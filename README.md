# mhke-mod

Moorhuhn Kart Extra as a mod for Moorhuhn Kart: Open Source Edition, with 10 tracks, karts, powerups and effects converted from Moorhuhn Kart and Moorhuhn Kart Extra. The game ships it preinstalled and builds it from this repository. Every push to main also publishes a GitHub release with a zip per platform that you can put into the Mods folder of the game to install a newer build.

Track proportions are corrected against the original games' road widths and lap lengths relative to kart size. Outdoor, Scribble Blocks, Kart Track, Moon and Docklands were enlarged ×1.495; Desert, Canyon, Ice, Volcano and Graveyard were reduced ×0.748 to remove the import's inconsistent scale groups. Item boxes retain their size and ground clearance; racing lines, collision, grids and track distances follow the corrected geometry, and lighting was rebaked. This changes lap times and invalidates old track ghosts.

## Fireball

The fireball floats above the road and spins upright like a kart, not end over end. Its local-X spin pivot is oriented upright, with the mesh counter-rotated to retain its neutral pose. The visual uses scale 0.5377778, a third larger than the previous 0.40333334, and its collision radius grows with it from 0.2 m to 0.26666668 m so the bigger ball still meets walls and roofs at its surface; the 1 m kart hit radius is unchanged. Its straight-line chase speed is 54.1875 m/s, 1.5 times the previous 36.125 m/s. This is the speed whenever it is not closing in on the leader: the close-pursuit and ahead-waiting speeds below follow the leader's top speed and stay as they were. The half-speed wall escape scales with it. The flight sound has a 2 m minimum distance and 60 m maximum distance.

It launches forwards even while looking backwards, retaining the kart's actual forward-facing release angle rather than immediately snapping to the course tangent. Near-perpendicular or wrong-way facing falls back to forward-course release. An angled shot steers at 180 degrees per second for its first 0.3 seconds before normal 900-degree road steering takes over; aligned launches retain normal responsiveness. It then drives the main path and alternative routes toward the current race leader. For the first second it cannot target its launcher: if the launcher leads, it drives forwards without a target to create an escape gap. Other leaders are eligible immediately, including during that window. Once the window ends, the launcher can be targeted after the fireball has cleared its body. Navigation projects onto the solved drivable line and advances the look-ahead in world metres, preventing orbiting where raw track distances bunch up at Luna's kinks. Route handovers follow the smoothed road geometry instead of waiting to cross raw path-node planes. Other fireballs do not block it, and its lifetime is 60 seconds, twice the previous 30.

Its height comes from the ground under it, probed from below any roof: a probe started above a low ceiling, such as Rome's arcades, would land on the roof's top and pin the shot against its underside. Under a roof lower than the hover height it flies as high as the roof leaves room for. Ground more than 1 m below its hover level is a gap: it crosses gaps the karts jump, holes in road colliders and pits beside the road level, like a jumping kart, for up to 12 m, then follows a real drop down no steeper than it advances (Castle's level changes). Fall zones only catch karts, so over them it has no ground. Leaving a ramp or a barrier top it travels level before descending, and ducks under a lintel by descending first; climbing stays a single sweep, so walls still stop it. Where the solved line runs through scenery the karts steer around, such as the pillar inside Rome's arcade, it aims up to 2.4 m to the nearer side instead of ramming it after every wall escape.

A wall impact briefly stops the fireball for 0.14 seconds, then gives it 0.35 seconds of half-speed swept escape movement along and away from the wall. Another face encountered during recovery changes the escape heading without restarting the stationary pause. The shot then resumes normal pursuit instead of teleporting onto the racing line or remaining pinned. Launch placement, deflection and online corrections all stay collision-swept; walls neither consume the fireball nor produce a water pop.

Inside 4 m of the leader's rear chassis extent it eases toward 95% of that kart's normal, unboosted top speed. Standing off from the body keeps normal cruising safe, while a brief partial slowdown lets the fireball catch up. More than 12.75 m ahead of the leader, it creeps at 3 m/s in 0.2-second steps every 0.5 seconds. Once the leader enters that nearby band, it immediately maintains 85% of the leader's normal top speed without coast pauses, using the same swept road navigation and wall collision. Only the far-ahead creep uses the fixed-step pulse approximation of the original throttle/coast behavior. Lap-aware progress distinguishes waiting from a finish-line crossing. A dead or respawn-protected leader does not stop its route traversal and cannot consume it.

An eligible leader entering the fireball's side/front cone is intercepted directly, including from the rear quarter before the leader's root passes. Interception requires the same route, a clear line of sight, a planar range and path gap within 10.625 m, and a heading difference no greater than 135 degrees. It turns at up to 2700 degrees per second instead of the road-following 900, with turn braking evaluated after the committed interception turn so the reaction starts immediately. The rear 90-degree wedge stays outside that cone. Physical sweeps and chassis contact still decide movement and hits; the larger interception cone is not a larger hit radius.

These behaviors were rechecked statically in the original MHKE-XXL.exe: 0x41dba0 selects the nearby leader-pressure speed, 0x41de5c switches its steering aim to the leader, and 0x41efc0 checks squared planar distance and relative heading. The original uses 300-unit pressure bands and a 250-unit interception radius. Spawn 0x436270 forwards the launch actor's transform and position into kart creation; collision handler 0x41f1a0 sets a contact flag, which the driving code at 0x41db55 uses to enter timed stuck recovery at 0x41da6c. This supports the transient collision/recovery pattern, not a claim of measured original runtime orientation or timer values. The metre ranges use a chosen 0.0425 conversion; speed shares, launch steering, wall timers and interception turn rate remain deliberate port tuning.

Close pursuit gently weaves from side to side, with an aim offset up to 0.65 m and a 2.4-second period. This is physical, swept movement rather than an offset of the model: the road edges constrain it, and it fades out before contact so a brief slowdown still lets the fireball catch the leader. Waiting and dead/respawn-protected targets do not enable the weave.

Touching an eligible leader's chassis applies the boil and consumes the fireball in the same physics step, including stationary overlaps, swept passes, wall pauses, moving recovery and swept online corrections. Locking on adds no further hit delay. Walls still block contact.

A kart hit adds a small animated blue-white water pop before the steam takes over: a glossy bubble squashes, ruptures into a rim with short droplets, then fades. It uses one stationary camera-facing particle and a padded 4x4 RGBA flipbook over 0.36 seconds, ending in two transparent frames. Its material renders after the steam while retaining normal opaque depth testing. The hit is reserved before applying gameplay effects so synchronous or late confirmations cannot duplicate the pop or steam. Lifetime expiry and rejected uses do not produce the water animation.

A hit boils the leader for 2.5 seconds: both kart and driver soften into a warm, rippling pool, with a translucent white-blue water sheen, white steam and water droplets. Bubbles boil across the sheen instead of stripes: MHK/Lit places at most one bubble in each kart-local cell of two layers (cells a third and a fifth of the body height), each at a random spot with a radius of 22–42 % of its cell, swelling, holding and popping on its own random cycle of 0.55–1.3 seconds (the finer layer runs 1.3 times faster), with a bright film edge and a small glint; a fifth of the cells stay empty. The bubble films blend up to 85 % towards the water colour, so they read clearly over the thinner sheen. Karts from mods show it once those mods are rebuilt with this SDK, because mod bundles carry their own copy of MHK/Lit. Both particle systems radiate outward from upward-facing cones, with extra world-up velocity carrying them higher without increasing sideways spread. Their velocity axes use matching TwoConstants modes, with X/Z kept at zero. Full deformation takes 0.708 seconds without shortening the gameplay penalty. The visual body scales uniformly to two-thirds around its cached base, without changing the chassis, and the pool sags by a quarter of that height (MHK/Lit's MHK_MELT_SAG), so the melted kart ends at half its normal height instead of flat on the road; its base still spreads out. While grounded and facing the road, a blended visual-only lift keeps the conservative full-melt envelope at least 0.02 m above the support plane inferred from wheel contacts, including a tilted body pose. It reuses contact data without extra raycasts and also works without shrinking. The visual-body transform and existing property blocks keep mod-bundled kart and driver materials working unchanged. Other kart and driver animations pause while the deformation and particles continue. There is no spin-out or character death animation. The effect respawns the kart when it ends; deformation, visual scale and position, water overlay, expanded renderer bounds, particles and animation pauses are restored when it ends, is cancelled or the kart respawns.

The attached steam and water systems generate particles only during active deformation: emission stops when effect progress reaches meltCompletion, about 0.708 seconds after an Extra fireball hit. StopEmitting preserves existing puffs so they fade naturally instead of being cleared at the molten transition. A refreshed hit restarts the same systems for its new active melting interval. The molten pose, water sheen, animation pause and 2.5-second gameplay penalty remain unchanged while emission is stopped.

Hits and lifetime expiry begin with 110 large, soft steam particles, including hits confirmed online. The independent plume spawns 40 particles per second during a 0.7083333-second window, matching the authored active melting interval rather than the molten hold. Existing puffs keep their lifetimes and fade after emission stops. Steam has a 159-particle capacity, reserving one for the kart-impact water pop so combined impact capacity remains 160. Puffs start at sizes 1.26–1.98 before growing and fading. A narrow 20-degree cone, 0.35 emission radius and 3.5 world-up velocity keep the cloud upright with limited sideways spread. The particles use the white steam material, zero gravity and translucent shading. Both effects destroy themselves after playback. Walls still do not consume the fireball; scene cleanup and server-rejected uses stay silent.

The shared white-steam material uses BoilSteamDense.png, a 512-pixel RGBA sprite with a dense billowing core and irregular transparent edges, for both the end burst and the two attached boiling systems. RGB stays white in transparent texels to avoid dark filtering fringes. Impact particles start at 0.18 alpha before the existing lifetime fade; the stronger visibility comes from texture coverage and opacity, not additional particles. The texture is mipmapped, clamped, GPU-compressed and not CPU-readable.

## Audio

Music (including menu/title/highscore cues) is normalized per song to -16 LUFS. Effects use one attenuation-only gain per source game, matching group medians to the quietest source while preserving intentional differences between cues; borrowed clips follow their source game's gain. Decoded peaks stay below 0 dBFS. Local cue gains and track music volumes are unchanged.

## Track variants

Every track can be driven in reverse. Every track except Moon has a time of day option, Graveyard is at night by default. Ice has snow, Desert and Canyon a sandstorm, and the other tracks except Moon rain. The overlay definitions are in each track's `Variants` folder, the overlay scenes (`<track>.night.unity`, `<track>.day.unity`, `<track>.rain.unity`, `<track>.snow.unity`, `<track>.sandstorm.unity`, `<track>.reverse.unity`) next to the track scene, the cover map for rain (`<track>.cover.asset`) beside them and the generated night, day, rain, snow and sandstorm skies in `Textures/Sky`. Night overlays darken the sun and ambient light and turn the karts' headlights on; rain adds rain with thunder, wet uncovered ground and less grip outdoors; snow adds snowfall and a little less grip outdoors; a sandstorm adds thick dusty fog and blowing sand. Reverse overlays turn the speed pad arrows round.
